Interpretative Interface No. 04 GPT‑2 small · PCA + UMAP

Halftone.

Halftone print reproduces continuous tone by translating smooth gradients into a field of dots — small marks for light areas, larger marks for dark ones. This interface borrows that logic for a different kind of continuous concept: the high-dimensional space of GPT‑2’s residual vectors. Since those 768-dimensional vectors cannot be seen directly, the prototype first flattens them into two-dimensional projections, then turns their movement and clustering into halftone fields. What appears as a printed pattern is therefore a compressed trace of how hidden states travel, gather, and change across the network.

The concept

What is the projected residual stream?

Inside GPT‑2, every token carries a 768-dimensional vector that gets revised as it passes through twelve transformer layers. Each layer reads the current vector, decides what to add, and writes a small update back. Stacking those vectors gives every token a trajectory through a very high-dimensional space — far too many dimensions to look at directly.

We compress those trajectories down to two dimensions, using two different methods. PCA finds the directions where the collected vectors vary the most and projects everything into that shared 2D space, making it useful for seeing broad movement between layers. UMAP builds another shared 2D layout that tries to keep nearby states close together, making it useful for seeing where residual states cluster.

A halftone field then samples each projection on a regular grid. At each grid position, the prototype estimates what is happening nearby — for PCA, the average layer-to-layer movement and the direction it’s heading; for UMAP, the local density of projected states. The result is not the 768-dimensional vector itself but a printed surface made from its projected motion and density.

The interaction

What you will do

  1. 01 Enter. A short poem is preset in the specimen text, but you can replace it with your own input.
  2. 02 Print. Press the print button. The backend tokenizes the text, runs GPT‑2 small on it, collects every token’s residual vector at every layer, projects them down with PCA and with UMAP, and returns the two flat fields for the page to print.
  3. 03 Read. The left plate shows movement: each ellipse points along the local direction of trajectory flow, and its size grows with how far hidden states travel through that region. The right plate shows clustering: large dots where many projected states gather, small dots where the projection is sparse. Hover any mark to see the nearest token-layer state.
  4. 04 Toggle. Beside each plate’s caption is a small plate / trace / overprint switch. Plate shows the halftone, trace replaces it with the underlying token trajectories — one colored polyline per token, threaded through twelve layers, with a dot at each layer, and overprint stacks the trace on top of the halftone for direct comparison.
